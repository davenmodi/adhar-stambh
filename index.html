Three statement pieces

Each pillar has one thing people will remember. Everything else in that part supports it.

Mandir · peace

The Shanti room

A short chaos hallway drops visitors into a sealed, glowing room: a backlit carved jali wall with three arches, three framed photos, a glossy white floor that reflects it, and a soft kirtan instrumental. No screens. Groups of about 8 for 90 seconds.

BuildReady-made PVC or MDF jali sheets painted ivory, framed 6–8 in off the wall with warm LED strip behind. Foam arches and pillars. Blankets behind ivory fabric for sound.

Shastra · wisdom

Floating pages

Three long scrolls of scripture, written top to bottom with verses in Gujarati and English. Two hang between the glass cases in the left column, facing the 6 ft aisle. The third hangs on the NFC wall between the two TVs, right where the aisle ends.

BuildScrolls about 1.5 × 9.5 ft on translucent voile or backlit fabric, dowels top and bottom, the pair in the left column hung from one black T-post hidden between them, the wall scroll from a bar on the wall top.

Sant · living it

The Sant pillars

Five tall panels stand in a straight row down the middle of Sant. You walk up the photo side first, then turn and come back down the other side, where each back explains one idea: What is a Guru?, The Guru Parampara, Pramukh Swami Maharaj, Mahant Swami Maharaj and Living the teachings.

Build4 plywood box panels, 2 ft wide, 8 in deep and 7 ft 8 in tall, painted black, with inset prints on both faces and a small picture light on top of each face. Only 16 in between them, so the row reads as one piece; people walk around the ends, not through.

Supporting pieces

Mandir

Chaos hallway

About 9 ft of cramped hallway, black from floor to ceiling, with one stream of notifications and photos running along the walls at eye level under two cold flicker bulbs. It doubles as the Shanti queue.

Mandir

Three booth kits

BKY is an L in the corner by the hallway, with twice the display wall. Better Living Sessions is on the side wall right outside the Shanti exit, and BAPS Charities is on the north wall. Volunteers stand and talk with visitors; each lead decides what happens in their booth.

Shastra

Scripture spine wall

Six giant spines like a library shelf. Every other one opens (1st, 3rd and 5th) to show the teaching, a story, and what it means in everyday life.

Shastra

Three glass cases

Shikshapatri and Swamini Vato at the ends of the left column, and Satsang Diksha across the aisle beside its world record panel, each in its own case and spotlight. The rest of the right side of the aisle is a rope line.

Shastra

Satsang Diksha World Record

A freestanding black-and-gold panel beside the Satsang Diksha case: “ONE WISH. THOUSANDS INSPIRED.” A large photo of the mass recitation, and 12,723 children reciting together on 2 February 2026, a Guinness World Record. Shastra learned. Shastra remembered. Shastra lived.

Shastra

Two NFC stations

Side by side on one wall as an “Ask the Shastras” spot. Tap a question card on a tablet and the answer comes up on the TV above, so the whole group can read it.

Sant

Vicharan wall

A TV on the wall loops Mahant Swami Maharaj’s vicharan since 2016: the globe turns and the route draws itself stop by stop while the distance counts up. Words around the screen give the totals. Nothing to touch; people stop and watch.

Sant

City at night

Wrapped around the south-east corner, where the walk turns, a dark foam skyline: “AWAKE FOR ALL OF US”. Each of six towers shows a time and one glowing window. On the ledge below, a card for each night tells where Mahant Swami Maharaj was and who he was praying for. The point: while the world sleeps, he stays awake for all of us.

Sant

Birthday wall

Next to the vicharan wall: a TV on the wall with an iPad on a stand in front. Type your birthday and see where Pramukh Swami Maharaj or Mahant Swami Maharaj was that day, with words around the screen that explain it.

All three

Names in the pieces

No banners. Mandir is carved into the jali over the Shanti room door, Shastra is the first word on the first scroll, and Sant is lettered under the photo on the middle Sant pillar, each in English and Gujarati.

Notes before you build

  • WallsPlywood in 4 × 8 ft modules on 2×4 frames, painted before setup, bolted together and braced with kickers, since nothing anchors to the hall floor. About 131 ft of outer wall and about 25 ft inside. Confirm which outer walls the hall or neighbouring sections already give you.
  • Walls and floorThe model assumes 8 ft walls from standard sheets. The gym floor gets black loop carpet (about $1 a sq ft, 12 ft wide rolls). Ask the facility what it allows on the gym floor, use only gaffer tape, and put felt pads under every wall foot.
  • Shanti roomConfirm with the venue that a covered room is allowed (flame-retardant fabric, sprinklers, exits). At about 8 people every 2 minutes it handles about 240 people an hour, and the hallway holds the line.
  • Floating pagesGet venue approval for the T-post or ceiling rigging, and sign-off on the verses before printing. Order the fabric scrolls early: dye-sub printing takes about a week.
  • NFCEach station is a tablet (the tap point) with a 45–50 in TV above, connected by a wired HDMI adapter rather than wireless casting. One web page and one working card by day 5. If not, put QR codes on the same cards so nothing is reprinted.
  • CrowdsAbout 6 ft of clear walking space everywhere. Places where people stop, like the booths, spines and NFC stations, sit outside that line.
  • Lighting14 clamp-on spots and 11 small picture lights at 3000K on dimmers, plus LED strip behind the jali. Hall lights off; exit signs stay on.
  • Booth leadsEach lead gets a 6 ft wall, a ledge, one spot and one outlet. No speakers or mics, nothing freestanding in the walkway, and content shown to you by a set date.
  • Vicharan wallThe route page has to run offline, full-screen and on autoplay, and loop by itself all day. Check every figure on the wall graphic against the vicharan record before printing.
  • Still openThe birthday archive (where Swami was each day), the three Shanti room photos, the final wording of the six city-at-night cards, and whether the entrance and exit really sit on opposite 30 ft ends.
  • TimelineOrder prints, jali sheets, foam pieces, plinths, glass cases and tablets in the first 3 days. Week 2 is painting, setup and testing. The Media assets tab in the 2D view lists every print, file and piece of writing to make; give each one an owner this week.

What changed from V1

  • 40 × 30 ft instead of 45 × 50 ft, as one open room with no walls between the three parts.
  • The walk now runs Mandir, then Shastra, then Sant.
  • The projector chaos-to-calm cycle became a short poster hallway into a backlit jali room with music.
  • The text lantern became hanging scripture scrolls. A spine wall was added and the NFC stations went from 6 to 2, each with a TV above to show the answer.
  • Each part is named inside its own statement piece instead of on signs.
  • The light tunnel and moving-portrait gallery were cut. A vicharan wall was added beside the birthday wall on the east wall: a TV looping Mahant Swami Maharaj’s travels since 2016. The closing wall on the north wall was removed; the birthday wall is now the last stop before the exit.
  • The whole layout was reflected top to bottom: the entrance from Mulyo is now bottom left and the exit top right. Every piece keeps its size and spacing.
  • The bicycle and book plinths were removed. The south wall of Sant became the city at night: six towers, six night prasangs.
  • The split portrait became five Sant panels in a straight row: photos on one side, information on the other. The exit moved to the bottom-right so the walk passes both sides.
  • BKY, Charities and Better Living became booth kits run by their activity leads.
